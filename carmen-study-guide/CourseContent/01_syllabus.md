## Course Description

Vibe coding is the practice of building software by describing what you want to an AI assistant, then steering, testing, and fixing what comes back. It is fast, fun, and occasionally catastrophic. This course teaches you to do it *well*: writing clear specifications, reading code you did not write, testing before trusting, and shipping something that still works on Monday.

You will not be graded on how much code the AI wrote. You will be graded on whether you understand it, can defend it, and can fix it when it breaks. The AI is your pair programmer, not your lawyer.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Explain** what vibe coding is, where it works well, and where it fails, including its effect on speed, quality, and maintainability.
2. **Write** precise prompts and specifications that include requirements, constraints, inputs/outputs, and examples.
3. **Read and critique** AI-generated code for correctness, clarity, efficiency, and style.
4. **Test and debug** AI-generated code using unit tests, edge cases, and systematic debugging rather than re-rolling the prompt and hoping.
5. **Identify and mitigate** security, privacy, and licensing risks in AI-assisted development.
6. **Iterate and document** a development process, including a prompt log and decision record, to build and ship a small working application.
7. **Evaluate** when to use AI assistance and when to write code by hand, and justify that decision.

## AI Use Policy

This is a course about using AI, so you are expected to use it. The rules:

- **Always allowed:** AI assistants for assignments and the final project, with a **prompt log** submitted alongside your work.
- **Never allowed:** submitting code you cannot explain. If the TA asks you what line 42 does and the answer is "the AI wrote that," you will receive a gentle but firm zero on that line.
- **Exams:** closed AI. You will be asked to read, trace, and write code by hand. Bring a pencil and your own brain.
- **Fabrication:** inventing prompt logs, fake test results, or "I tested this" when you did not is an academic integrity violation.

## Late Policy

- Assignments lose 10% per day, up to 3 days. After that, they are not accepted.
- Two free "grace days" are available for the semester. No explanation needed.
- Documented emergencies and accommodations are handled case by case. Email the instructor early.

## Academic Integrity

Collaboration on concepts is encouraged. Collaboration on code must be disclosed in your submission. Copying another student's work or their prompt log is not permitted. When in doubt, disclose.
